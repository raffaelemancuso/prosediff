# Mass and energy

In 1905 Einstein showed that a body at rest has the energy $E = m c^2$, which is
enormous for everyday masses. The so-called "inertial mass" is conserved in
every closed system -- a result checked in 1905--1907 by later work.[^1]

Three consequences follow:

- Nuclear reactions release **a small part** of the rest mass.
- A hot body weighs slightly *more* than a cold one.
- Light carries momentum as well as energy.

| Quantity | Symbol | Unit |
|----------|--------|------|
| Energy   | E      | joule |
| Mass     | m      | kilogram |

For a moving body the full relation is

$$E^2 = (p c)^2 + (m c^2)^2$$

as shown in [the original paper](https://example.org/einstein1905).

[^1]: The experiments were refined throughout the twentieth century.
